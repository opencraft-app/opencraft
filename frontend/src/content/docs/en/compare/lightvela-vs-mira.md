# LightVela vs Mira: Which is best to run your personal AI Agent?

## Summary

**Choose LightVela if you want one personal Hermes Agent shaped around your routines, reusable Skills, Automations, and the messaging Channels you choose.** Choose Mira if Telegram is where your work and communities already live, and you want to turn conversations into follow-ups, summaries, reminders, and actions.

Both products go beyond a chat window, but they start from different centers of gravity: Mira is a Telegram-first assistant, while LightVela is a configurable personal Agent that can grow into a wider workflow system.

![LightVela compared with Mira for running a personal AI agent](../images/lightvela-vs-mira.png)

## At a glance

| What matters | LightVela | Mira |
| - | - | - |
| **Best for** | A personal, long-running AI Agent | A personal or team assistant inside Telegram |
| **Core experience** | Configure one Agent around your routines and preferences | Ask an Agent to understand chats and act from the conversation |
| **Setup focus** | Skills, Automations, memory, persona, models, and Channels | Telegram chats, group context, connected tools, and recurring actions |
| **Everyday value** | A consistent Agent that keeps working beyond one conversation | Less context switching between chats, tasks, and connected tools |
| **Strongest use case** | Personal workflows that should keep improving over time | Chat-based follow-ups, briefings, content, and team coordination |

## What changes in everyday use

The practical difference is not which product gives the better answer to one prompt.
It is where the Agent lives in your day and how much of its operating model you want to shape.

With LightVela, you configure a hosted Hermes Agent around how you work, then keep talking to the same Agent as your needs evolve.
You can install reusable [Skills](../configuration/skill.md), schedule [Automations](../configuration/automation.md), choose a [model](../configuration/model.md), and connect the Channels you actually use.
That turns a useful one-off interaction into a workflow that can keep working for you.

You might ask for a daily briefing, a recurring watchlist, a content draft, or a reminder without rebuilding the process every time.
LightVela is designed around that continuity: one Agent, one evolving setup, and one place to refine how it should help.

[Mira](https://mira.tg/) starts from a different premise.
It is built natively inside Telegram, where you can use it in personal chats, group chats, and channels without opening a separate application.
Mira presents itself as an Agent that can remember context, summarize conversations, track decisions, connect external tools, and turn messages into real actions.

That makes Mira especially natural when the source of the work is already a conversation.
A team can add Mira to a group, ask what was decided, turn an agreement into follow-up tasks, or request a briefing without leaving Telegram.
The experience is closer to delegating work inside a shared chat than configuring a separate personal workspace.

## Where LightVela is the better fit

LightVela is the better fit if you want your AI Agent to become a consistent part of your personal operating system.
You are not only asking an assistant to react to the latest messages.
You are shaping how the Agent should behave, what it should know, which capabilities it can use, and when it should act.

You might want an Agent that checks information every morning, watches competitors, turns product updates into social drafts, or summarizes a research topic each week.
The useful part is not one perfect answer.
It is being able to turn a useful interaction into something that keeps working.

That is where Skills and Automations matter.
A Skill gives your Agent a reusable capability, while an Automation lets routine work happen on a schedule instead of relying on you to remember the prompt at exactly the right time.
You can start with a morning briefing, add a research Skill, and send the result through the Channel you are most likely to see.

LightVela is also the more direct choice if you care about configuring the Agent's role rather than accepting a fixed product experience.
You choose the model, the behavior you want to encourage, the capabilities you want to add, and the Channels where the Agent should show up.
The [LightVela overview](../01-overview.md) explains how those pieces fit together.

The tradeoff is that LightVela is intentionally centered on one evolving personal Agent.
If your main need is to understand and act on busy Telegram conversations, Mira may feel more immediate.

## Where Mira is the better fit

Mira is the stronger option when Telegram is already the place where your work, communities, and decisions happen.
Its official site describes a Telegram-native assistant that can help with content, inboxes, crypto monitoring, shopping, travel planning, reminders, and connected tools.
The key advantage is that the context is already present in the chat where the request begins.

Mira is also a natural fit for teams and communities.
It can follow who said what, summarize a long group discussion, surface open decisions, and help turn conversation into assigned follow-ups.
That is useful for product teams, founders, creators, and community operators who spend more time in Telegram than in a dedicated task system.

The same Telegram-first model can support daily briefings and recurring workflows.
You can ask for a summary at the right time, delegate a research task, or connect services such as GitHub, Notion, and Google Drive from the chat experience.
Mira's official documentation also highlights a broad tool and integration layer, so the assistant can move from an answer to an action without asking you to switch contexts.

The boundary is focus.
Mira is optimized around the Telegram interface and the social context inside it.
If you want to design one Agent's persona, model, Skills, Automations, and Channels as a longer-term personal system, LightVela gives you the more explicit control surface.

## Which should you choose?

Choose LightVela if you:

- **Want one personal Agent:** You want a hosted Hermes Agent that becomes more useful as you refine it.
- **Want continuity:** You want the same Agent to stay available and keep your setup over time.
- **Prefer configurable routines:** You want reusable Skills and recurring Automations around your own habits.
- **Want flexible access:** You want to reach your Agent through the Channels that fit your workflow.
- **Want direct control:** You want to manage the model, persona, capabilities, and workflow shape.

Choose Mira if you:

- **Live in Telegram:** Your personal work, team decisions, or community activity already happen in chats.
- **Need conversation memory:** You want an Agent to understand what people said and what still needs to happen.
- **Want fast delegation:** You want to turn a message into a reminder, task, summary, draft, or connected-tool action.
- **Need group collaboration:** You want one shared assistant that can participate in personal and group conversations.
- **Prefer minimal setup:** You want to start chatting with an Agent without configuring a separate workspace first.

My take: **LightVela is the better fit for one personal AI Agent that grows around your routines. Mira is the better fit when Telegram is the operating surface for your conversations, work, and follow-ups.**

If you want to compare Mira with another assistant built around a different execution model, see [LightVela vs Grok Bot](./lightvela-vs-grok-bot.md).

If your question is “Which Agent should live inside my Telegram conversations?”, Mira is probably the clearer answer.

If your question is “Where should I run an AI Agent that I keep shaping and using every day?”, LightVela is the cleaner fit.
